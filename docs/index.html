Linking an HP ZGX Nano (NVIDIA DGX Spark platform) and an AMD Ryzen AI Halo for llama.cpp RPC

Interactive guide v1.0.1 · matches guide v1.4.0 · companion to builtbyfood/spark-halo-llama-rpc · every figure was measured on real hardware, November 2026

One direct 10GbE cable joins the two machines. The Halo (Windows, Vulkan) holds the models and serves the API; the ZGX Nano, called "the Spark" here, lends its GPU when it is connected.

Topology

Security. The llama.cpp RPC server has no authentication and no encryption. Bind the worker only to the direct-link address. Never bind it to 0.0.0.0 or a LAN address.

Steps

How big is the model?

Rule of thumb from the free memory each machine reported with nothing else loaded (106.3 GiB on the Halo's GPU, 118.7 GiB on the Spark). Only a 35 GiB and a 153 GiB model were tested. Leave room for context, and stop other models first when a model is close to a limit.

Layouts

35B under sustained load

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q8_0, 30 minutes at each level, 400 streamed tokens per request, temperature 0. No errors in any run.

35B benchmark, four layouts

llama-bench -ngl 99 -p 512,2048 -n 128 -r 2, tokens per second.

A model too big for either machine

Qwen3.5-397B-A17B at UD-Q3_K_S, 153 GiB, split across both machines with every other model stopped.

This is the case splitting exists for: neither machine can hold the file, together they can.

Jumbo frames (MTU 9000)

A 4 to 6% gain on generation for the 35B placed entirely on the Spark, no change in load time, and no gain for the 153 GiB split (14.03 against 14.06). The default MTU of 1500 is fine.

What happens when the cable is pulled

Tested mid-session with the model running through RPC. Walk through it:

Takeaway. Nothing recovers by itself. The head exits about 2.5 s after the pull and the worker is left stuck. Recovery is: reconnect, restart the worker, then restart the head. Allow about 1.5 minutes, 69 s of it the head's model load. Keep a Halo-only fallback for the gap.
